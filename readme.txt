=== The Injil ===
Requires at least: 6.6
Tested up to: 6.8
Requires PHP: 7.4
Template: twentytwentyfive
License: GPLv2 or later

Custom child theme of Twenty Twenty-Five built for one site: The Rightly-Guided Injīl at injil.help. It carries that site's pages, content, fonts and licences, and is not intended for use on any other site.

== Install ==
1. Install and keep Twenty Twenty-Five (the parent). Do not delete it.
2. Upload this folder to wp-content/themes/injil-help/ and activate "The Injil". Set Settings > General > Site Title to "The Rightly-Guided Injīl" (the header and footer read it from there).
3. (Automatic) On activation the theme creates all 176 pages listed under Theme Options > Content, sets pretty permalinks, and fills them with content. Nothing else is needed; the site is complete for visitors as it stands.
4. Optional: create categories for the parts (The Five Gospels, Paul of Tarsus, Jesus in the Qur'an, The Gospel According to Islam, Resources) if you intend to publish posts.
5. To rebuild a page by hand: the section pages (/gospels/, /paul/, /quran/, /gospel-according-to-islam/, /resources/, /answers/) use the "Section index" template with their "Section index: ..." pattern (or "FAQs page"); /method/ is the "Criterion statement" pattern, /corrections/ the "Corrections log", /the-islamic-dilemma/ the pillar-page pattern; every other page is built from inc/data/ and is restored from Theme Options > Content.

== Starter content ==
The theme ships its own pages and creates them on activation (inc/starter-content-pages.php), 176 in all: the pillar page /the-islamic-dilemma/, its answer to the argument's secular form (/the-islamic-dilemma/secular-form/) and its answer to Pfander's Balance of Truth, the source of the Christian polemic (/the-islamic-dilemma/balance-of-truth/), /method/, /corrections/, /answers/, the five section pages and their entries (the four gospels, the Gospel of Thomas with its full text in two translations, its set-aside sayings and the sayings-order test, the synoptic problem, the five discrepancy tables; six Paul of Tarsus pages; six Qur'an pages and the full compilation of passages at /quran/passages/; the 92 pages of the classical commentaries under /quran/commentaries/; the Introduction and 27 sections of The Gospel According to Islam and how it was composed; timeline, glossary, source library, the manuscripts of the extracanonical gospels, the borrowing charge, objections and the replies to named articles), the bonus epistle, plus About, Terms, Privacy, Contact, Credits and Sitemap. Appearance > Theme Options > Content lists every page and creates any that are missing (for example after you delete one). Pages are published; edit them in the block editor. The articles are concise and sourced, and every page is complete when it is created: there is no placeholder text anywhere in the theme. On the first activation the theme also bins WordPress's own sample post, sample page and sample comment (if they were never edited) and replaces the "Just another WordPress site" tagline, so a new install is ready for visitors as it stands. After a theme update, every starter page whose text changed in the new version is rewritten from the theme (once per version, on the first admin screen load), except pages you have edited since the theme wrote them: each page the theme writes carries a hash of its content, an edited page no longer matches, and it is kept and marked "edited, kept on update" on the Content tab. Pages whose text did not change are left alone, and no revision is kept for the rewrite. Theme Options > Content has an "Update all pages from the theme" button that rewrites every page on demand, edited ones included, behind a confirmation.

== Content data ==
Everything a visitor reads is kept as data, not code: the articles, the gospel, the Qur'anic passages, the commentaries and the list of pages are JSON files in inc/data/, and the page layouts with their copy are block patterns in patterns/. That is why text can be corrected, translated or reviewed without touching PHP, and why the site can be rebuilt from the theme alone. The folder holds:
* The starter pages, one file per part of the site (the pillar page and the measure, the gospels, Paul of Tarsus, the Qur'an, the gospel, the bonus chapter, resources, and the site's own pages).
* The Narrative of Jesus, Son of Mary (six chapters, 27 sections, 235 notes), the section titles and the Introduction.
* The Qur'anic passages on Jesus: 13 surahs, 45 passages, 145 verses, with their headings and footnotes.
* The Gospel of Thomas sections and the early Christian witnesses to the substitution, woven into the gospel where they belong.
* The classical commentaries on the verses about Jesus, Mary, Zachariah and John: al-Tabari, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, Tanwir al-Miqbas, al-Jalalayn, al-Durr al-Manthur and al-Sa'di, translated in full (about 315,000 words).
The page bodies use a small plain-text markup (headings, lists with colour marks, quotations, claim labels, footnotes, FAQ entries and folded blocks); README.md documents it for anyone editing the files.
== Structure ==
Modelled on apostleofdoom.org: a title-page hero; each part of the study as a block with a gloss (Greek for New Testament parts, Arabic for Qur'anic parts, each with its verse), an intro, a "Read first" link and numbered entries; a band of FAQs with "Full evidence" links; a footer of study, reference and method links.

== What it adds ==
* Patterns (Appearance > Editor, category "injil.help"): the front-page sections and FAQ band, the parts grid, the pillar page, the measure, the five section indexes and the FAQs page, how this gospel was composed, the corrections log, the five discrepancy tables, the manuscripts table, the sayings-order test, the Qur'an verse index, and the building blocks (answer capsule, claim labels, gospel verse, objection and reply, FAQ item). Insert any of them on a new page.
* Block styles in the editor's Styles panel: ornament separator; claim labels (Scholarly consensus, Minority view, Islamic position); source labels (Qur'an, NT-attested, Editorial); answer capsule; gospel verse; objection and reply; criterion box; discrepancy table; FAQ item (which also feeds the page's FAQ rich result).
* Page templates: "Section index" for index pages and "Wide page (tables)" for pages with wide comparison tables.
* The Gospel According to Islam set like a printed Bible: a running head, the section numeral, an argument line, numbered verses, the Qur'an's own verse numbers in green, two columns on wide screens, and the notes at the foot of the text in small type. Choose two columns, one column or plain paragraphs under Theme Options > Content > Gospel text layout. The verse breaks are markers in the data and renumber themselves when the text is edited.
* Structured data for search engines on every page, switched off automatically when Yoast, Rank Math or SEOPress is active.
* A share row under posts and pages: X, Facebook, WhatsApp, Telegram, Reddit, LinkedIn, Bluesky, Threads, Pinterest and LINE, with Copy link and, where the browser allows, a button that opens the device's share sheet. Choose the networks and the label under Theme Options > Sharing.
== Fonts ==
Bundled in assets/fonts and declared in theme.json, so every face is available in the editor's font picker. Roles:
* Sabon Next LT: body text. EB Garamond: headings. Kagnue: site title, the titles of the section index pages and home sections, the large numerals and the cover title. Evangelion CPA: labels, kickers, navigation.
* SBL Greek, SBL Hebrew, SBL BibLit: biblical languages and transliteration (also applied by lang="grc", lang="he").
* KFGQPC Hafs Uthmanic (the King Fahd Complex face): reserved strictly for Qur'anic text; every Qur'anic gloss in the header, footer and section heads uses it. Do not use it for anything that is not the Qur'an. Noto Naskh Arabic: hadith. Amiri: all other Arabic. Dubidam Arabic, Arslan Wessam: Arabic display.
* Estrangelo Edessa: Syriac. Noto Sans Cuneiform: cuneiform. Special Elite: the corrections log. Ferments, Angel: available, unused by default.
The class names and language codes that select each face are listed in README.md.

Licensing: Sabon Next LT (Linotype) and Kagnue (Youthlabs Studio) are commercial faces licensed to the site owner; a new site must hold its own licences for them. Angel is freeware and asks for its README to be kept with the font (assets/fonts/Angel-README.txt). The rest are OFL or free for use; keep their licence files.

== SEO: "the Islamic Dilemma" ==
* The pillar page is /the-islamic-dilemma/, created for you. Its excerpt is its meta description: keyword first, under 160 characters.
* The theme sets the home title to "The Islamic Dilemma, Answered | <site name>" and the pillar title to "The Islamic Dilemma, Answered: Qur'an 5:47 and the Injīl", and writes the meta description and social-preview tags for every page. Change the titles and description under Theme Options > SEO. If Yoast, Rank Math or SEOPress is active, the theme steps back and you set the same values in the plugin.
* Structured data: every page carries the markup Google documents for sites like this (the organisation and author, the site search, the page type, breadcrumbs, article details, the gospel as a book, the licence of every manuscript image, and the FAQs). Fill in the author name, profile links and logo under Theme Options > SEO.
* FAQs: every main page carries six questions and answers written the way people search; the front page carries six and the FAQs page twelve. Each feeds that page's FAQ rich result.
* The keyword appears in the home kicker and lede, the home dilemma section, the first nav item, the first two FAQs and the footer. Link to /the-islamic-dilemma/ from every new article that touches Qur'an 5:47, taḥrīf or the identity of the Injīl, with varied anchor text.
* The default social image can be replaced under Theme Options > SEO.
== Images ==
Manuscript images load from Wikimedia Commons, each with its credit and a link to its Commons page in the caption. All are public domain except the Kairouan Qur'an photograph (Richard Mortel, CC BY 2.0), which must keep its credit. Every image carries a descriptive alt attribute (what it is, its date and holding institution) and a short title; keep that pattern for new images, and write alt text for the subject, not for decoration. WordPress adds lazy loading itself. The default Open Graph image is a bundled copy of the Birmingham Qur'an manuscript (assets/images/og-default.jpg, public domain, 1280 × 833); replace it under Theme Options > SEO. To host the manuscript images yourself, download from the linked Commons page into the Media Library and swap the URL. Readers' browsers contact upload.wikimedia.org; mention this in the privacy policy. Twenty-eight article pages also carry a plate listed in inc/data/images.json, and the same image becomes the page's featured image once the host has fetched it (six pages per admin screen load; a failed fetch is retried after a day). To change a page's image, edit its entry: the Commons file name without "File:", the alt text, the caption and the credit. Use only public-domain or CC0 files, and check that the file exists.

== Branding ==
* Brandmark: assets/brandmark.svg (an open codex with rays of light, Qur'an 5:46). It appears as the link before the site title in the header and at the top of the footer's first column (class ih-mark; the footer adds ih-mark-foot).
* Favicon: if no Site Icon is set under Settings > General > Site Icon (or in the Site Editor), the theme outputs assets/brandmark.svg as an SVG favicon. A Site Icon takes precedence unless Theme Options > Branding is set to force the brandmark or to use a custom favicon URL. Some older browsers ignore SVG favicons, so set a PNG Site Icon for full coverage.

== Theme Options ==
Appearance > Theme Options is a tabbed page:
* Colours: nine palette overrides with colour pickers; empty keeps the default.
* Branding: ornament layer (Rich, Subtle, Off) and the reveal animation; show or hide the footer brandmark; always use the brandmark as favicon even with a Site Icon; custom favicon URL.
* Sharing: show or hide the share row, on posts and/or pages; which networks, in order; the row's label.
* Content: lists every starter page with its status, creates missing ones, and rewrites all of them from the theme on demand.
* SEO: theme SEO output automatic, always on or always off; home title and description; pillar page title; default social image URL; structured data: author name and URL, profile links (sameAs), organization logo URL.
The page's own colours follow the Colours tab.

== Ornament ==
Icons, pattern, banners and motion are a separate decorative layer, drawn so that every decoration takes its colour from the palette and follows Theme Options > Colours.
* A star lattice behind the page, a colour wash and pattern on the front-page hero, a tinted banner with a watermark icon on each section page (book for the gospels, quill for Paul of Tarsus, star for the Qur'an, lamp for The Gospel According to Islam), a flourish under each section title.
* Entry cards carry a section icon, lift on hover and show an arrow; headings carry a small star; saying badges carry an icon; manuscript images are matted and framed; the footer carries the pattern.
* Theme Options > Branding > Ornament: Rich, Subtle (fainter pattern) or Off; a switch for the reveal animation; and Parallax depth: Strong (default), Gentle or Off. As the page scrolls the lattice, the hero's layers, the section banners, the manuscript plates, the card grids and the footer pattern move at different speeds. Visitors who have asked their device for reduced motion get none of it.
== Customising colours ==
The nine colours of the site can be changed without code under Appearance > Theme Options > Colours; every pattern, badge, icon and banner follows, on the site and in the editor. The defaults: Parchment (page), Ink (text), Qur'anic green, Rubric red, Gold, Lapis, Vellum and Rule (panels and lines) and a muted grey. Leave a field empty to keep the default. The same colours can be edited in Appearance > Editor > Styles > Colors; README.md names the tokens for developers.
== Documentation ==
This file is the user-facing manual that WordPress and Git Updater read. The repository also carries README.md (the technical reference for theme developers, web developers and hosts: architecture, data, hooks, server requirements, workflow), SSOT.md (the single source of truth: every fixed fact about the theme, its slugs, data files, options, tokens and rules), CHANGELOG.md (the full version history; the Changelog section below mirrors it) and UPGRADING.md (the roadmap: improvements, features and content to add, the work that would make the theme fool-proof, and notes on updating an existing site).

== Releasing ==
Bump Version in style.css and INJIL_HELP_VERSION in functions.php together, add the entry to CHANGELOG.md and to the Changelog section here, note anything an existing site must know under "Updating an existing site" in UPGRADING.md, merge to main, and tag the commit with the same number (for example v3.4.0). Git Updater reads the header (GitHub Theme URI, Primary Branch: main) and offers the update.

== Design ==
After Ahmad Shafaat, The Gospel According to Islam (Vantage Press, 1985): chapter-and-verse layout, verse numbers in the margin, rubric-red chapter heads, notes beside the text. Palette: parchment, ink, Qur'anic green, rubric red, gold, lapis.
The ornament layer follows Ismail al-Faruqi, Divine Transcendence and Its Expression (ABIM, Kuala Lumpur, 1983), pp. 28-32: stylisation and geometry as the refusal to let anything in nature stand for the divine, and the endlessly repeating pattern as the expression of God's inexpressibility. Hence the eight-pointed star lattice, the line icons, and no figurative image of anything sacred; the only pictures are manuscripts.
