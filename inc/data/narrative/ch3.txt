@chapter 3 The Ministry in Galilee
@section 9
And Jesus also preached among the publicans who worked for the Roman rulers to collect tax and custom. And one publican named Levi became his disciple. And Levi made him a great feast in his house, and there was a great company of publicans and of others that sat down with them. But the scribes and the Pharisees murmured against his disciples, saying, Why do ye eat and drink with publicans and sinners. And Jesus, answering to what they murmured, said unto his disciples, They that are whole need not a physician, but they that are sick.
I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentence[^84] And some of the scribes and Pharisees also said, Why do his disciples eat and dine and not fast often as did John. And Jesus said, Whereunto shall I liken the men of this generation? and unto what are they like? They are like unto children sitting in the marketplace, and calling one to another, and saying, We piped unto you and ye did not dance, we mourned and ye did not weep. For John came neither eating bread nor drinking wine, and they say, He hath a demon.
And I have come eating and drinking and they say, Behold a gluttonous man, and a wine-bibber, and a friend of publicans and sinners. But wisdom is justified by all her children.[^85] And the disciples of John reported to him of all these things.[^86] And John sent two of his disciples, and said unto him, Art thou he that should come with me?[^87] or look we for another? When the men were come unto him, they said, John hath sent us unto thee, saying, Art thou he that should come with me? or look we for another?
And Jesus answered and said, Blessed is he, if he does not doubt me.[^88] I came not to destroy the sabbath or fasting but only those who make them lords over man.[^89] And I came not to destroy the law or the prophets, but to confirm them.[^90] And when the messengers of John were departed, he began to speak concerning John, saying, I tell you among those born of women there is none greater than John the Baptist. And all the people that heard him, and the publicans, justified God, being baptized with the baptism of John.
But the Pharisees and the lawyers rejected the counsel of God against themselves, being not baptized of him.[^91]
@section 10
Then came together unto him certain of the Pharisees and the scribes from Jerusalem.[^92] And when they saw some of his disciples eat bread with unwashen hands, they found fault. For the Pharisees and many of the Jews held that if food is eaten with unwashen hands, it is defiled. And they asked Jesus, Why walk not thy disciples according to the tradition of the elders, but eat bread with unwashen hands? He answered and said unto them; Well hath Esias said of you hypocrites, as it is written, This people honoureth Me with their lips, but their heart is far from Me.
Howbeit in vain do they worship Me, and their fear toward Me is taught by the precept of men.[^93] For ye teach the traditions of men as commandments of God[^94] and lay aside what God commanded. For Moses said, Honour thy father and thy mother; and, Whoso curseth father or mother, let him die: But ye say, If a man shall say to his father or mother, It is Korban, that is, a gift, by whatsoever thou mightest be profited by me; he shall be free. And ye suffer him no more to do aught for his father or his mother.[^95]
Making the word of God of none effect. And when he had come away from the people and entered into a house, he received the Spirit and testified and said unto his disciples, Verily, verily, I say unto you, No prophet from Israel will be sent to uproot their traditions. For no man putteth a piece of a new garment upon an old; and no man putteth new wine into old bottles; but new wine must be put into new bottles. For this cause will my heavenly Father raise a prophet from the midst of another nation, and put His word into his mouth, that He may uproot every plant which He hath not planted;[^96] that He may give unto the world a new wine that men of all nations may quench their thirst.
After these things it came to pass that there came to Jesus a Greek woman, Syrophoenician by nation: Her daughter had an unclean spirit, and she besought Jesus that he would cast it out of her. But he answered her not a word; and his disciples came and besought him, saying, Send her away; for she crieth after us. But he answered and said, I am not sent but unto the lost sheep of the house of Israel.[^97] Howbeit I know another man of God, and she can go to him, for he taketh devils out of all kinds of people.
@section 11
And Jesus continued to travel and preach for the spiritual renewal of Israel; and when he was preaching in Bethsaida, people brought to him a blind man that he may heal him. And he spit on his finger and rubbed the eyes of the blind man, and asked him if he saw ought, And he looked up and said, I see men as trees walking. After that he put his fingers again upon his eyes, and made him look up; and by God’s healing power he was restored and saw every man clearly.[^98] And one day Jesus was traveling and he came to a well.[^99]
And being wearied with his journey, sat on the well; and it was about the sixth hour. Then there cometh a woman to draw water: Jesus saith to her, thou hast six pitchers of water at home, how many more thou needest? The woman was amazed that Jesus knew what she had in her house and saith unto him, Sir, I perceive that thou art a prophet. Jesus saith unto her, Today for thy first meal, thou ate butter and honey after a long time, but to what Jacob and the prophets and I call my people is better sustenance than this water and butter and honey.
And it can give thee what the two husbands thou have hadst could not give thee. The woman was therefore amazed all the more that Jesus also knew her past. She saith unto him, Sir, what is it that Jacob and the other prophets and thou call us to and is better than this water and the butter and honey I ate today and the two husbands I have had? Jesus answering unto her saith, Worship ye the One True God who created me and you and who provides us with our daily sustenance, Worship ye Him in spirit and in truth,[^100] and besides Him worship none, for it can neither profit nor harm.
And upon this came some of his disciples (who had gone away to the city to buy meat), and the woman then left, and went her way to the city. And she saith to the people, Come, see a man who told me all things that I ever did.[^101] Then they went out of the city and came unto him, and Jesus preached them what he had preached to the woman, and they were amazed at his doctrine, but some said, Have we not heard this before? Jesus answered and said, Ye have heard it but not received it. For, else why take ye the traditions of the elders like unto the commandments of God? And why make ye the scribes your lords besides God?[^102]
And again they heard but did not understand, and they began to ask him, Tell us how many pitchers of water we have at home? What did we eat today? But Jesus said unto them, A prophet can do nothing without God’s leave, and he can see nothing which he is not shown. And I do no miracle but as a sign to those who will believe and fear the coming day of the Lord. And he went his way with his disciples and some of those who heard him believed on him at that very hour.
@section 12
And he came back to Capernaum and there one day cometh to him one of the rulers of the synagogue, Jairus by name. And he besought him greatly, saying, My little daughter lieth at the point of death. Come and lay thy hands on her that she may be healed and may live. And Jesus went with him; and many people followed him. And when he cometh to the house of Jairus, he seeth the relatives of the girl weeping and wailing greatly. And her father, when he saw this, also started to weep and wail. But Jesus told him, The damsel is not dead but sleepeth.
And he was in the chamber where she lay; and the power of the Lord to heal came upon him and he saith unto her, By God’s leave, I say unto thee, Arise. And the damsel arose and walked: for she was of the age of twelve years. And they were astonished with a great astonishment. And he charged them that something should be given her to eat[^103] And it came to pass that when these things were happening, Herodias plotted to have John the Baptist killed. For she hated John because that he would not fear anyone but God and reproached her for her adultery.
And when Herod’s birthday came, he made a big feast to his lords, high captains, and chief estates of Galilee. And when his guests were eating and drinking, Herodias sent her daughter Salome that she may dance before them and please them. And Herod, being exceedingly pleased by Salome, promised with an oath to give whatsoever she would ask. And Salome ran to her mother, saying, What shall I ask? And Herodias said, The head of John the Baptist in a charger. Now when the king heard what Salome wanted, he was sorry for his oath because he did not want to kill John; for the multitude counted him as a prophet, and he feared them.
Nevertheless, for the oath’s sake, and them that sat with him at meat, he would not reject her. And so the king sent an executioner and commanded his head to be brought: and he went and beheaded him in the prison. And brought his head in a charger, and gave it to the damsel: and the damsel gave it to her mother. And his disciples came, and took up the body, and buried it, and went and told Jesus.[^104] Thus was John persecuted and killed for righteousness sake, for he preferred to suffer than bow before the power of wicked men.
So peace be upon John the day he was born and the day he was killed without a just cause, and the day he will be raised to life again.
@section 13
And when Jesus saw the disciples of John, he was moved with compassion toward them, because they were as sheep not having a shepherd.[^105] And one evening he went out into a mountain to pray, and continued all night in prayer to God.[^106] And when it was day, he called unto him his disciples and of them he chose twelve.[^107] Simon (whom he also named Peter or Caiphas, meaning Stone) and his brother Andrew. And James and John, the sons of Zebedee (whom he called Boanerges, which is, the sons of thunder) And Philip, and Bartholomew, and Matthew, and Thomas, and James the son of Alphaeus, and Thaddaeus, Simon the Canaanite, and Judas Iscariot.
And these twelve he chose that he might send them to preach, And to heal sicknesses, and to cast out devils. But Jesus knew not that one of them, Judas Iscariot, will betray him, That the world may know that the prophets can do nothing without the Lord’s leave and that they can see nothing which they are not shown. And Jesus sent forth the twelve, and commanded them, saying, Go not into the way of the Gentiles and into any city of the Samaritans enter ye not: But go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.[^108]
And provide neither gold, nor silver, nor brass in your purses, Nor scrip for your journey, neither two coats, neither shoes, nor yet staves: for the workman is worthy of his meat. And into whatsoever city or town ye shall enter, enquire who in it is worthy; and there abide till ye go thence. And whosoever shall not receive you nor hear your words, when ye depart thence, shake off the dust of your feet. Verily I say unto you, It shall be more tolerable for the land of Sodom and Gomorrha in the day of Judgement, than for that city.
And they went out, and preached, saying, Repent, for the kingdom of God is at hand.[^109] And they cast out many devils, and annointed with oil many that were sick, and healed them. And Jesus also departed thence to teach and preach.[^110]
